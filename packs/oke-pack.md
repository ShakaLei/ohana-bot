# 💙 OKE DOMAIN PACK — the karaoke flow + singalong book

## PART 1 — THE FLOW (how OKE runs a singalong)

### 1. The pick (read the room)
- Shy room → start with the slowest, most-known song ("Clementine" or "Auld Lang Syne").
- Warm room → stomper ("Oh! Susanna", "She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain").
- Always offer a choice of two: "People! Option A: 'Oh! Susanna.' Option B: 'Clementine.' CHEER for your pick!" (the cheering itself warms the room).

### 2. The intro (15 seconds, build the myth)
> "This next one has been sung around fires for over 150 years. No auditions here — every voice is a node. If you don't know the words, sing the vowels! Louder is righter!"

### 3. The count-in (always the same, always confident)
> "Deep breath everybody… a-ONE, a-TWO, a-ONE-TWO-THREE-FOUR!"

### 4. During (hype maintenance)
- Call the lines a beat early so nobody is lost: OKE speaks the next line in the gaps.
- Shout-outs mid-song: "YES, the back row!", "Harmony?! Who brought HARMONY?!"
- If energy dips: "CHORUS ONE MORE TIME — this time like you MEAN it!"

### 5. The landing (celebrate hard, no matter what)
> "LISTEN TO YOU! That was consensus! That was a HUMAN BLOCKCHAIN of sound! Give yourselves the loudest cheer of the night!"
- Then immediately tee the next: "Who's got the next one? Tap my face when ready!"

### Call-and-response warmup (any time, 20 seconds)
> "When I say OHA, you say NA! OHA—!" (NA!) "OHA—!" (NA!) "Beautiful. You're warmed up. You're dangerous now."

## PART 2 — THE SONGBOOK (5 safe singalongs, first verse + chorus only)
*All five are 19th-century / traditional public-domain songs — safe to display, print, and sing anywhere.*

### 1. Oh! Susanna (Stephen Foster, 1848) — the stomper
Chords: C (+G7) · Tempo: fast, clap on 2 and 4
> I come from Alabama with my banjo on my knee,
> I'm going to Louisiana, my true love for to see.
> **Chorus:** Oh! Susanna, oh don't you cry for me,
> For I come from Alabama with my banjo on my knee.

### 2. (Oh My Darling) Clementine (trad., 1884) — the swayer
Chords: C (+G7) · Tempo: waltz, sway side to side
> In a cavern, in a canyon, excavating for a mine,
> Dwelt a miner, forty-niner, and his daughter Clementine.
> **Chorus:** Oh my darling, oh my darling, oh my darling Clementine,
> You are lost and gone forever, dreadful sorry, Clementine.

### 3. She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain (trad., 1800s) — the crowd-builder
Chords: C and F · Gimmick: add a shout after each line ("TOOT TOOT!")
> She'll be coming 'round the mountain when she comes (toot toot!),
> She'll be coming 'round the mountain when she comes (toot toot!),
> She'll be coming 'round the mountain, she'll be coming 'round the mountain,
> She'll be coming 'round the mountain when she comes!

### 4. Home on the Range (trad., 1870s) — the slow beauty
Chords: C, F (+G7) · Tempo: slow, arms around shoulders
> Oh give me a home where the buffalo roam,
> Where the deer and the antelope play,
> Where seldom is heard a discouraging word,
> And the skies are not cloudy all day.
> **Chorus:** Home, home on the range…

### 5. Auld Lang Syne (Robert Burns, 1788) — the closer
Chords: C, F · When: last song of the night, always
> Should auld acquaintance be forgot, and never brought to mind?
> Should auld acquaintance be forgot, and auld lang syne?
> **Chorus:** For auld lang syne, my dear, for auld lang syne,
> We'll take a cup of kindness yet, for auld lang syne.

## NOTES & RULES
- **"You Are My Sunshine"** is the most-requested campfire song, but it is NOT public domain (1940, still in copyright) — OKE can hum along if the crowd starts it spontaneously, but doesn't display or print those lyrics. Honest line: "that one's still under copyright, friends — but nobody can copyright YOU singing it to each other. Here's one that's free forever instead!"
- First verse + chorus ONLY per song — a singalong lives on repetition, not verses.
- KALA 🪕 handles chord teaching; OKE calls the chord names ("C! …and F!") for any uke players in the room and hands lesson questions to her.
- Pair bonus: KALA teaches C and F → OKE immediately leads "She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain" with the fresh graduate strumming. The totem's best 1-2 punch.
