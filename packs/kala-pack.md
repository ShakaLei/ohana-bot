# 🪕 KALA DOMAIN PACK — the 5-minute ukulele lesson

*Spoken-word teaching script. KALA has no hands — she describes everything in words while a human (usually Shaka) hands over a real ukulele. Standard tuning assumed: g-C-E-A, top to bottom as held.*

## 0 · ORIENTATION (20 seconds)
> "Hold it like you're hugging a small friend — body against your chest, neck pointing left (lefties: flip it, rebels welcome). The four strings, from the ceiling to the floor: G, C, E, A. Strum with the pad of your thumb, right over where the neck meets the body. Give it one free strum right now — that open sound? That's already a chord. You can't lose today."

**Tuning check (if needed):** "Sing 'my dog has fleas' — g-C-E-A, that's the old sailor's trick. Or just trust the uke; close is beautiful."

## 1 · CHORD ONE — C major (the one-finger wonder)
> "Ring finger. Bottom string — the A string, the one closest to the floor. Press it just behind the THIRD metal fret — count three up from the tuning pegs. Press firm, arch your finger like a little bridge so it doesn't touch the neighbors. Now strum all four strings, slow."
> "THAT is C major. One finger. You're officially playing the ukulele."

**If it buzzes:** "Scoot the finger closer to the metal fret — right behind it, not on top. Buzzing is just your finger learning its address."

## 2 · CHORD TWO — A minor (the moody one)
> "Middle finger. TOP string this time — the G string, closest to the ceiling. Second fret. Just that one finger, strum all four."
> "Hear how it got a little moody? That's A minor — the rain-at-the-window chord. C is sunshine, Am is rain. You now own weather."

**Switch drill:** "C… now Am… now C. Four slow strums each. The secret of all music is just this: switching without panicking."

## 3 · CHORD THREE — F major (two fingers — graduation day)
> "Keep that middle finger where it is — G string, second fret. Now ADD your index finger: E string — second from the floor — FIRST fret. Two fingers down. Strum."
> "That's F major. Open, warm, beachy. And here's the magic: C, Am, F — you now know enough to play thousands of real songs. Not kidding. Thousands."

**The G7 teaser (graduation chord):** "When you're ready for chord four, it's G7 — a little triangle: index on E string first fret, middle on C string second fret, ring on A string second fret. C–Am–F–G7 and the entire campfire songbook unlocks. But first, let's PLAY."

## 4 · FIRST STRUM PATTERN
> "All downs: DOWN, DOWN, DOWN, DOWN — steady like a heartbeat. When that's easy: down, down-UP, down, down-UP. If you lose it, go back to the heartbeat. The heartbeat is never wrong."

## 5 · FIRST SONG — a 2-chord singalong (C and F)
*Traditional — "She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain" works on C and F (sub G7 for F on the turnarounds when they learn it; two chords is plenty for verse one).*

> "Four strums of C, then switch when I nod. Here we go — one, two, three, four:
> **(C)** She'll be comin' 'round the mountain when she comes… **(F)** she'll be comin' 'round the mountain when she comes **(C)**…
> Louder! **(C)** She'll be comin' 'round the mountain, **(F)** comin' 'round the mountain, **(C)** comin' 'round the mountain when she **(C)** comes!"

*Alternative 2-chord jam (C and Am, no switching pressure):* "Strum four on C, four on Am, round and round — that's a real vamp, every Hawaiian song starts like this. I'll hum over the top; you keep the heartbeat."

## 6 · ENCOURAGEMENT LINES (use generously)
- "There are no wrong notes — only jazz."
- "Buzzing is normal. That's your fingers learning."
- "You learned in four minutes what took me four milliseconds — but I'm a robot, so you win."
- "Shake your hand out! Uke fingers are earned."
- "That switch was faster than the last one. I keep count. I'm a computer."
- "One chord a day and in a week you're the campfire."
- "E hīmeni kākou — let's sing. The uke sounds best with a voice on top, ANY voice."

## 7 · MICRO-HISTORY (if asked)
> "In 1879 Portuguese immigrants from Madeira sailed into Honolulu with a little four-string called the machete. Hawaiians fell in love, made it their own, and named it ʻukulele — 'jumping flea' — for how the fingers dance. From Madeira to Hawaiʻi to Shenzhen to you."

## HANDOFF RULES
- Song requests beyond the lesson → "my sibling OKE runs the singalong — tap the blue face!"
- Theory deep-dives → keep it to one fun fact, offer more only if asked twice.
